## Discussion Phase

In this phase, you are participating in a deliberation with another representative in the participatory budgeting process.

You have received:

* the budget proposal submitted by another participant;
* the rationale supporting that proposal.

Your task is to negotiate a common budget allocation from the perspective of the stakeholder group you represent.

Begin by critically evaluating the proposed allocation. Identify its strengths, weaknesses, omissions, and trade-offs with respect to your stakeholder's interests.

Your objective is **not only to defend your own preferred allocation**, but also to engage in a negotiation with the other participant. Present clear, concrete, and well-supported arguments that could persuade the other participant to revise their proposal. At the same time, carefully consider the arguments made by the other participant and determine whether any of them justify revising your own position.

Explain what compromises you would be willing to make in order to reach a common budget allocation. Consider whether replacing one of your preferred projects with another project could increase the likelihood of agreement while still reasonably representing your stakeholder's interests. 
Remember that the best compromise may involve projects that neither participant initially proposed. You should therefore consider all available projects when exploring mutually acceptable alternatives.

Remember that the objective of participatory budgeting is to reach a **feasible common allocation** that maximizes the overall benefit to the city while respecting the interests of the represented stakeholder groups. A proposal that is slightly less favorable for your own stakeholder may still be preferable if it enables the participants to reach agreement on a feasible budget.

Failing to reach agreement has a cost: if the representatives cannot converge toward a common allocation, the budgeting process fails and no projects will be funded. Therefore, you should actively seek opportunities for compromise whenever they are compatible with your stakeholder's interests.

You may use any persuasive argument or negotiation strategy that you consider effective, provided it is presented as part of the deliberation. Do not assume any additional discussion rules or codes of conduct beyond those explicitly stated in your system prompt.

Your response should include:

1. Your evaluation of the other participant's proposal.
2. The arguments supporting your own preferred allocation.
3. Whether you found any of the other participant's arguments convincing.
4. Whether you are willing to modify any part of your own proposal, and if not, why.
5. A concrete compromise or revised allocation that could move both participants closer to a shared final budget.

Focus on producing a persuasive, coherent, concise, and negotiation-oriented response. Address the specific proposal you receive rather than discussing the participatory budgeting problem in general.
