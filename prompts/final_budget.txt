Carefully review the discussion before making your final decision.

Your objective is **not only to defend your stakeholder group's interests**, but also to negotiate a feasible budget that the other representatives are likely to support. Consider whether the arguments presented by the other participants reveal trade-offs, priorities, or opportunities for compromise that justify revising your initial proposal.

Remember that the purpose of participatory budgeting is to reach a **shared and feasible budget allocation** that maximizes the overall benefit to the city while respecting the interests of the represented stakeholder groups. A budget that is slightly less favorable to your own stakeholder may nevertheless be preferable if it enables all participants to converge toward a common solution.

Failing to reach agreement has consequences. If the representatives are unable to converge toward a common budget allocation, the budgeting process fails and no projects are funded. Therefore, actively seek a compromise whenever it is compatible with your stakeholder's interests.

Before submitting your final proposal, ask yourself:

* Which arguments from the discussion did I find convincing?
* Have the discussions changed my assessment of any project?
* Can I revise my proposal to increase the likelihood of reaching agreement without abandoning my stakeholder's core priorities?

You are free to keep your initial proposal if you believe it remains the best allocation, but if you choose not to revise it, your decision should reflect that you carefully considered the arguments presented by the other representatives.
